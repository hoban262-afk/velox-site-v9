What Did Lilly Present at EASD 2026 and Publish in The Lancet?

Eli Lilly’s dedicated retatrutide (LY3437943) symposium ran at the 62nd Annual Meeting of the European Association for the Study of Diabetes in Milan on Wednesday 30 September 2026, 08:30–09:30 CEST.[1] Trade coverage reports that the full TRIUMPH-2 results were published simultaneously in The Lancet under the title “Retatrutide in adults with obesity and type 2 diabetes (TRIUMPH-2)”.[2]

This is the first time the complete dataset has been open to independent scrutiny. Lilly’s July 2026 topline release gave headline figures only; a peer-reviewed paper adds the methods, prespecified analyses and dose-level safety tables that researchers use to judge a trial. For the earlier stages, see our coverage of the TRIUMPH-2 and TRIUMPH-3 topline and the EASD preview.

Transparency note: the figures below are taken from Lilly’s releases and trade-press summaries of the presentation. We have not independently reviewed the full-text paper, so check numbers against the primary publication.

What Was the TRIUMPH-2 Design?

Phase 3, 80 weeks, presented 30 Sept 2026
TRIUMPH-2: Retatrutide in Adults With Obesity/Overweight and Type 2 Diabetes

Population: 1,152 adults with obesity or overweight and type 2 diabetes, baseline A1C about 7.7%.

Arms: once-weekly retatrutide 4 mg, 9 mg or 12 mg versus placebo.

Registry: ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05929079

Retatrutide is a single peptide that agonises the GLP-1, GIP and glucagon receptors. Its pharmacology is covered in our mechanism guide; this article focuses on the published trial readout.

What Were the Dose-Level Efficacy Results?

At 80 weeks, mean body-weight change was reported as 12.7% at 4 mg (about 29.8 lb), 19.1% at 9 mg (45.4 lb) and 20.8% at 12 mg (49.6 lb; 22.5 kg), against 4.0% with placebo.[3][4] A1C fell by up to 1.6 percentage points versus 0.2 points on placebo. Lilly also reported that more than half of participants no longer met the criteria for obesity and that up to 40% reached an A1C in the normal range.[2]

Secondary biomarker endpoints at the top dose were reported as triglycerides −37.0%, non-HDL cholesterol −16.5%, systolic blood pressure −9.3 mmHg and hs-CRP −51.2%.[3] These are trial readouts in a licensed-pharma study, not statements about any research-use product.

What Did TRIUMPH-2 Report on Tolerability by Dose?

Gastrointestinal adverse events predominated. Discontinuations due to adverse events were reported as 3.8% (4 mg), 11.6% (9 mg) and 7.7% (12 mg) versus 4.9% with placebo.[3][4] The non-monotonic pattern — the middle dose discontinuing more than the top dose — is the kind of detail that only becomes interpretable with the paper’s titration and timing data.

Dysesthesia, an abnormal-sensation signal also seen in earlier retatrutide trials, was reported at 4.5%, 5.6% and 7.3% across the three doses versus 0.7% with placebo, generally mild to moderate, with the majority resolving during the trial.[4] That is a monotonic dose pattern, which sharpens the mechanistic question; we track it in our dysesthesia explainer.

Does the Publication Change Retatrutide’s Regulatory Status?

No. Retatrutide remains investigational with no marketing authorisation from the FDA, MHRA or any regulator. Lilly’s stated plan, from its July 2026 release, is a Biologics License Application in Q1 2027, and the separate biologic-versus-drug classification dispute is before the Seventh Circuit (argued 24 September; no ruling reported). See our FDA approval timeline guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

What did TRIUMPH-2 report at EASD 2026?

Lilly's 30 September 2026 EASD symposium in Milan presented full Phase 3 data from TRIUMPH-2 (n=1,152, obesity/overweight with type 2 diabetes): mean weight change of 12.7%, 19.1% and 20.8% at 4 mg, 9 mg and 12 mg at 80 weeks versus 4.0% with placebo, with A1C reductions of up to 1.6 percentage points, as reported by Lilly and trade press. The results were reported as simultaneously published in The Lancet.

How did discontinuations differ by dose in TRIUMPH-2?

Discontinuations due to adverse events were reported as 3.8% (4 mg), 11.6% (9 mg) and 7.7% (12 mg) versus 4.9% with placebo. Gastrointestinal adverse events predominated.

What did TRIUMPH-2 report about dysesthesia?

Dysesthesia incidence was reported as 4.5%, 5.6% and 7.3% at 4 mg, 9 mg and 12 mg versus 0.7% with placebo. Events were described as generally mild to moderate, with the majority resolving during the trial.

Does the Lancet publication change retatrutide's regulatory status?

No. Retatrutide remains investigational with no FDA, MHRA or other marketing authorisation. Lilly's stated plan, from its July 2026 release, is to file a Biologics License Application in Q1 2027.
