What Has the MHRA's Criminal Enforcement Unit Uncovered Since October 2025?

Between October 2025 and May 2026, the MHRA's Criminal Enforcement Unit (CEU) dismantled three separate illegal UK operations manufacturing or distributing counterfeit injection pens marketed as retatrutide and tirzepatide — a Northampton warehouse, linked premises in Lincolnshire and Nottinghamshire, and a country estate, again near Northampton.[1][2] Across the three raids, officers recovered more than 14,000 doses of unlicensed weight-loss injectables, tens of thousands of empty pens awaiting filling, raw chemical ingredients, packaging lines and manufacturing equipment.

The pattern the MHRA describes is an escalating one: each raid targeted premises the agency links to the same wider criminal network, and the most recent operation, in May 2026, produced the largest single seizure the CEU has recorded.[1] None of the products seized had passed any regulatory testing, quality control or manufacturing licensing — they were sold to consumers as finished medicines with no verified content, purity or dosing accuracy.

What Happened at the October 2025 Northampton Warehouse Raid?

Criminal enforcement operation — MHRA Criminal Enforcement Unit
Northampton warehouse raid, October 2025

What happened: CEU officers, supported by Northamptonshire Police, raided a warehouse on the outskirts of Northampton over a two-day operation beginning 22 October 2025, identifying what the MHRA described as the UK's first known illicit weight-loss medicine manufacturing facility.[3]

What was seized: More than 2,000 unlicensed retatrutide and tirzepatide pens ready for dispatch to customers, tens of thousands of empty pens awaiting filling, raw chemical ingredients, sophisticated packaging and manufacturing equipment, and roughly £20,000 in cash suspected to be linked to medicines trafficking.[3]

Scale: Press coverage at the time described it as the largest single seizure of trafficked weight-loss medicines recorded by any law-enforcement agency worldwide, with an estimated street value of more than £250,000.[3]

Officials characterised the operation as a significant early win. Press reporting quoted Andy Morling, Head of the MHRA's Criminal Enforcement Unit, calling the result "a landmark result for the MHRA and a major blow to the illegal trade," and stating that stopping tens of thousands of potentially unsafe products from reaching the public had prevented a serious risk to health.[3]

What Happened at the February 2026 Lincolnshire and Nottinghamshire Raids?

Criminal enforcement operation — MHRA Criminal Enforcement Unit
Lincolnshire & Nottinghamshire raids, 25 February 2026

What happened: The MHRA, supported by Lincolnshire Police, Immigration Enforcement and Lincolnshire Trading Standards, searched a farm near Sleaford and a residential address in Grantham (Lincolnshire), plus a further Nottinghamshire property, as part of an ongoing investigation into an organised criminal group.[2]

What was seized: Almost 2,000 doses of unauthorised weight-loss medicines awaiting dispatch to customers, alongside manufacturing equipment, suspected pharmaceutical ingredients, packaging materials and commercial vehicles.[2]

Official framing: The MHRA's own press release describes this as disrupting a "second manufacturing facility" tied to the same network first identified in Northampton in October 2025 — language that positions the two operations as connected, not isolated incidents.[2]

What Happened at the May 2026 Raid — the MHRA's Largest Seizure Yet?

Criminal enforcement operation — MHRA Criminal Enforcement Unit
Country estate raid near Northampton, 28 May 2026

What happened: MHRA officers raided a country estate near Northampton and arrested two men, both aged 29, on suspicion of offences under the Human Medicines Regulations 2012.[1] The property is believed to have been used to manufacture, assemble and distribute unlicensed weight-loss medicines and other peptide products at scale.

What was seized: Around 12,000 doses of unlicensed weight-loss medicines — including retatrutide and tirzepatide pens — plus tens of thousands of empty pens ready for filling, raw chemical ingredients and packaging equipment, with a suspected value exceeding £250,000.[1]

Significance: The MHRA described this as its largest ever seizure of unlicensed weight-loss medicines, and the first of the three operations to result in arrests under the Human Medicines Regulations 2012.[1]

How Does This Fit the MHRA's Wider 2025 Enforcement Record?

These three raids are not isolated events. The MHRA's own published 2025 enforcement summary reported almost £45 million in illegally traded medicines seized across the year — nearly 20 million doses in total — including more than 5,000 illegally traded GLP-1-class weight-loss products, with the October Northampton warehouse raid cited as a headline case.[4] The same report noted the CEU worked with internet service providers to disrupt over 1,500 websites and social media accounts illegally selling medical products, and denied criminals access to more than £2.1 million in assets linked to the illegal medicines trade.[4]

Read together with our earlier coverage of Ireland's HPRA warning after a black-market retatrutide hospitalisation, and the MHRA's own Yellow Card adverse-event data for licensed GLP-1 medicines, these raids form part of a consistent regulatory picture across the British Isles: rapid growth in demand for GLP-1-class weight-loss compounds has been matched by rapid growth in illegal, unverified supply.

Why Is Illegal Manufacturing of Retatrutide and Tirzepatide Happening at This Scale?

High consumer demand, no UK licence for retatrutide

Retatrutide is not yet MHRA-approved for any use, while tirzepatide (Mounjaro) is licensed but expensive and supply-constrained, creating a gap that illegal manufacturers fill with counterfeit product marketed as either compound.

No verification of contents

None of the pens seized across these three raids had undergone any legitimate quality testing. The MHRA has previously found detained "GLP-1" products that did not contain the active ingredient claimed on the label.

Organised, not opportunistic

The MHRA's own language — describing the February 2026 raid as disrupting a "second manufacturing facility" — indicates these operations are treated as one connected criminal network under ongoing investigation, not unrelated one-off incidents.

These are finished consumer products, not research reagents. Every pen described in this article was manufactured and packaged to be injected by a consumer as a purported medicine, with no batch documentation, no third-party purity verification and no regulatory oversight of any kind. This is categorically different from an HPLC-verified research chemical sold in lyophilised form to a laboratory for in vitro study.
