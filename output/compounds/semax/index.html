Research overview

Semax is a synthetic heptapeptide derived from the ACTH(4-7) fragment of adrenocorticotropic hormone (the tetrapeptide Met-Glu-His-Phe), extended at the C-terminus with a Pro-Gly-Pro tripeptide to form the sequence ACTH(4-7)PGP. The Pro-Gly-Pro extension increases resistance to enzymatic degradation, improving the compound’s stability in experimental conditions. Importantly, Semax does not retain the steroidogenic activity of full ACTH — it does not stimulate cortisol production — and research has instead focused on its neurotrophic and neuroprotective properties in animal and cell culture models.

Preclinical research on Semax has concentrated on neuroprotection and cognition models. Studies in rodent models of cerebral ischaemia and oxidative stress have examined whether Semax modifies markers of neuronal damage and survival. Cell culture research has examined its effects on BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) production — BDNF is a key regulatory protein involved in neuron growth, maintenance, and synaptic plasticity. Studies have reported that Semax upregulates BDNF mRNA expression in hippocampal cell models, making it relevant to neurogenesis and synaptic plasticity research.

Semax has also been studied in the context of monoaminergic systems in rodent models, with researchers examining its influence on serotonin and dopamine activity in brain regions associated with cognition and mood. Its structural similarity to neuropeptides derived from ACTH places it within a broader research programme examining the neuropeptide-cognition interface. What is the relationship between Semax and ACTH in research?
Semax is derived from the ACTH(4-7) fragment (Met-Glu-His-Phe) of adrenocorticotropic hormone, extended with a Pro-Gly-Pro tripeptide. Full ACTH stimulates adrenal cortisol production via the MC2R receptor; Semax does not share this activity and does not bind MC2R. Research interest in Semax is focused on its distinct neurotrophic and neuroprotective properties — particularly BDNF modulation — rather than the hormonal functions associated with the full ACTH peptide. This makes Semax a useful tool for studying ACTH fragment neurobiology without the confound of HPA axis activation.
