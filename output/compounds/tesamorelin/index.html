Research overview

Tesamorelin is a 44-amino-acid synthetic analogue of full-length endogenous Growth Hormone Releasing Hormone (GHRH(1-44)), modified at the N-terminus with a trans-3-hexenoic acid conjugate to improve metabolic stability relative to native GHRH. Unlike CJC-1295 wo DAC — which corresponds only to the shorter GHRH(1-29) fragment — Tesamorelin encompasses the complete natural GHRH sequence, providing a research tool that more closely approximates the endogenous hormone’s structure. The N-terminal modification extends its half-life in experimental conditions while preserving the primary GHRH receptor binding interaction.

Preclinical and clinical research on Tesamorelin has focused on two principal areas: growth hormone secretagogue biology and visceral adipose tissue metabolism. As a GHRH receptor (GHRHR) agonist, Tesamorelin stimulates GH release from anterior pituitary somatotroph cells. Research examining the metabolic consequences of GH elevation via Tesamorelin has particularly focused on visceral adipose tissue — the metabolically active fat deposited around abdominal organs — with studies examining effects on adipocyte lipolysis, circulating lipid markers (triglycerides, LDL, HDL), and lean mass and adipose tissue distribution parameters in animal models.

Tesamorelin is also studied in the context of downstream IGF-1 (Insulin-like Growth Factor 1) signalling and in neurological research examining whether GHRH receptor agonism affects cognitive and CNS function markers in preclinical models, building on evidence that GH and IGF-1 receptors are expressed in hippocampal tissue. Its full-length GHRH sequence makes it a useful comparator with truncated GHRH analogues in studies examining receptor occupancy, activation kinetics, and downstream signalling profiles.
