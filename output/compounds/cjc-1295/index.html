Research overview

CJC-1295 without DAC (wo DAC) is a 30-amino-acid synthetic analogue of Growth Hormone Releasing Hormone 1-29 (GHRH(1-29)) — the minimally active N-terminal fragment of endogenous GHRH responsible for receptor binding and GH secretion. It incorporates several amino acid substitutions compared to native GHRH(1-29) (sermorelin) that improve its enzymatic stability and receptor binding affinity. The designation “without DAC” (without Drug Affinity Complex) indicates the absence of a maleimidoproprionic acid (MPA) moiety that would otherwise allow covalent binding to circulating albumin for extended half-life — making CJC-1295 wo DAC a shorter-acting GHRH analogue compared to the DAC formulation.

Research on CJC-1295 wo DAC centres on growth hormone secretagogue biology — the study of compounds that stimulate growth hormone (GH) release from the anterior pituitary via the GHRH receptor (GHRHR). In preclinical animal models, GHRH analogues have been examined for their ability to stimulate pulsatile GH secretion and to examine the downstream effects on circulating IGF-1 (Insulin-like Growth Factor 1), which mediates many of GH’s anabolic and metabolic effects in peripheral tissues. The shorter half-life of CJC-1295 wo DAC makes it useful for studies requiring acute, defined GH stimulation windows rather than prolonged elevation.

As a research tool, CJC-1295 wo DAC is frequently used in studies examining GHRH receptor pharmacology, GH secretion dynamics, and pituitary function in rodent models. It is also studied alongside GHRP (growth hormone releasing peptide) compounds in combinatorial protocols, as GHRH receptor agonists and ghrelin receptor agonists act synergistically on somatotroph cells to amplify GH release. What is the difference between CJC-1295 with DAC and CJC-1295 without DAC?
