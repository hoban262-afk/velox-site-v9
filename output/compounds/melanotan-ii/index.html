Research overview

Melanotan II (MT-2) is a synthetic cyclic heptapeptide and an analogue of alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH), a naturally occurring neuropeptide involved in skin pigmentation, energy regulation, and appetite signalling. Unlike α-MSH, Melanotan II incorporates a cyclisation between its second and seventh residues that substantially increases its binding affinity to melanocortin receptors and extends its metabolic stability in experimental conditions — properties that make it particularly useful as a research tool for studying melanocortin system pharmacology.

Melanotan II binds non-selectively to the melanocortin receptor family — specifically MC1R, MC3R, MC4R, and MC5R. These receptors are expressed across multiple tissue types and cell systems: MC1R is associated with melanin synthesis in melanocytes and is studied in the context of skin pigmentation and UV response; MC3R and MC4R are expressed in the central nervous system and hypothalamus and are linked to energy homeostasis and feeding behaviour in animal models; MC5R has been studied in relation to exocrine gland function and immune modulation. Preclinical studies have investigated Melanotan II’s effects across all four receptor subtypes.

Because of its multi-receptor binding profile, Melanotan II is used as a research probe across several disciplines including dermatological pigmentation research, metabolic pathway studies, and neuroscience. Its high receptor affinity and structural stability make it a useful comparator compound in studies examining selective versus non-selective melanocortin agonism.
