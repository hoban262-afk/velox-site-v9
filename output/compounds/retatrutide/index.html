Research overview

Retatrutide is a synthetic peptide engineered to act as a triple agonist at three distinct G protein-coupled receptors: the glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (GLP-1R), the glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor (GIPR), and the glucagon receptor (GCGR). This multi-receptor profile is structurally and pharmacologically distinct from single-agonist (GLP-1R only, e.g. semaglutide) and dual-agonist (GLP-1R/GIPR, e.g. tirzepatide) compounds — the simultaneous activation of all three metabolic receptor pathways provides researchers with a tool for studying combined receptor interactions in metabolic signalling that cannot be replicated with simpler agonist compounds.

Preclinical research on triple GLP-1/GIP/glucagon agonists has examined their effects on energy expenditure, adipose tissue lipolysis, hepatic glucose output, and pancreatic beta cell function. The GLP-1R component is associated with insulin secretion and gastric motility research; the GIPR component with insulin potentiation and lipid metabolism studies; and the glucagon receptor component with energy expenditure and hepatic glycogenolysis. Studies have examined how simultaneous agonism across all three receptors produces additive or synergistic effects on metabolic markers in rodent models, including adiposity, glucose tolerance, and lipid panels.

As the most pharmacologically complex compound in the Velox Peptides catalogue, Retatrutide is primarily of interest to researchers studying multi-receptor metabolic signalling, incretin biology, and energy homeostasis. Its availability in multiple vial sizes (10mg, 15mg, 20mg) and a 10mg 10-pack supports both cell culture and more extensive preclinical study designs. Velox Peptides supplies Retatrutide at ≥99% HPLC-verified purity as a lyophilised research reagent with batch certificate of analysis. Not for human or veterinary consumption.

How does Retatrutide differ from semaglutide or tirzepatide as a research compound?
Retatrutide is a triple GLP-1/GIP/glucagon receptor agonist, whereas semaglutide is a single GLP-1R agonist and tirzepatide is a dual GLP-1R/GIPR agonist. The additional glucagon receptor (GCGR) agonism in Retatrutide provides a distinct pharmacological profile for research, particularly for studies examining energy expenditure and hepatic metabolic pathways that are not fully accessible with GLP-1R or GLP-1R/GIPR agonism alone. This makes Retatrutide a useful research tool for comparative receptor pharmacology studies in metabolic research.
