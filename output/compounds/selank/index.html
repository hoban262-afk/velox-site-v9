Research overview

Selank is a synthetic heptapeptide with the amino acid sequence Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro (TKPRPGP). It was developed as a metabolically stable analogue of tuftsin (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg), an endogenous immunomodulatory tetrapeptide derived from immunoglobulin G. The appended Pro-Gly-Pro tripeptide at the C-terminus inhibits degradation by peptidases that would otherwise rapidly cleave the parent tuftsin sequence, extending the half-life of the compound in experimental conditions and making it a more tractable tool for research applications.

In preclinical animal research, Selank has been studied primarily in behavioural neuroscience models. Studies have examined its effects in anxiety models including the elevated plus maze, open field test, and conflict-based paradigms in rodents, with researchers investigating its interaction with GABAergic neurotransmission pathways. It has also been examined in relation to enkephalin metabolism — enkephalins are endogenous opioid neuropeptides involved in pain and stress response — with some rodent studies reporting that Selank inhibits enzymes responsible for enkephalin breakdown, thereby extending enkephalin activity in the synapse.

Beyond anxiety models, Selank has been studied for effects on memory consolidation and learning paradigms in rodents, and for its interaction with brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) expression. Its tuftsin-derived structure has prompted immunological research examining its effects on T-cell function and cytokine balance in preclinical models. What is the structural relationship between Selank and tuftsin?
Selank (TKPRPGP) is a synthetic heptapeptide analogue of tuftsin (TKPR), an endogenous immunomodulatory tetrapeptide derived from the Fc region of immunoglobulin G. This modification extends the compound’s suitability for experimental research while retaining its interaction with systems associated with the tuftsin parent structure.
