Research overview

Mots-C is a 16-amino-acid peptide encoded within the mitochondrial genome — specifically within the 12S ribosomal RNA (rRNA) gene — making it one of the very few biologically active peptides known to be encoded by mitochondrial rather than nuclear DNA. It was first identified in 2015 by researchers examining small open reading frames (sORFs) within the mitochondrial genome. As a mitochondrial-derived peptide (MDP), Mots-C occupies a distinct research category: it is an endogenous signalling molecule that appears to communicate mitochondrial metabolic status to other cellular compartments — a form of retrograde (mitochondria-to-nucleus) signalling.

Preclinical research on Mots-C has primarily focused on metabolic regulation. Cell culture studies have reported that Mots-C activates AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), a master sensor of cellular energy status that is activated when the AMP:ATP ratio rises. Downstream of AMPK activation, researchers have examined effects on glucose uptake, fatty acid oxidation, and insulin signalling in skeletal muscle cell lines. Rodent studies have investigated Mots-C administration in diet-induced metabolic dysfunction models, examining changes in lean mass and adipose tissue distribution parameters, glucose tolerance, and insulin sensitivity markers. Research has also examined its effects on the folate cycle and AICAR metabolism, suggesting involvement in purine biosynthesis regulation.

Beyond metabolic biology, Mots-C has attracted research interest in the context of ageing and cellular stress responses. Plasma Mots-C concentrations have been reported to change with age and metabolic status in human samples, prompting investigation of its role as a research marker. Its unique mitochondrial origin and systemic signalling properties make it a subject of growing interest in longevity biology, mitochondrial communication research, and exercise physiology studies. Why is Mots-C considered a mitochondrial-derived peptide (MDP)?
Mots-C is encoded within a small open reading frame (sORF) inside the 12S ribosomal RNA gene of the mitochondrial genome — not the nuclear genome like most proteins. Its classification as a mitochondrial-derived peptide (MDP) reflects this unique origin. Unlike nuclear-encoded proteins that are imported into mitochondria, Mots-C is translated within the mitochondrion and then travels to the nucleus and cytoplasm to exert regulatory effects. This retrograde signalling mechanism — mitochondria communicating metabolic state to the rest of the cell — is a relatively new area of cell biology that Mots-C has helped define.
