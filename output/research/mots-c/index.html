What is MOTS-C?

MOTS-c is a 16-amino-acid mitochondrial-derived peptide, encoded within the mitochondrial 12S ribosomal RNA, studied in preclinical models as a regulator of metabolic homeostasis through activation of AMPK. It is supplied strictly as a research reagent for in vitro use, not for human or veterinary consumption.

What makes MOTS-c unusual is its origin. Almost every peptide in the body is encoded by the nuclear genome; MOTS-c (Mitochondrial Open reading frame of the Twelve-S rRNA type-c) is instead encoded inside the mitochondrial genome itself. It belongs to a small, comparatively recently described class of mitochondrial-derived peptides (MDPs) that appear to act as signalling molecules carrying information from the mitochondria to the rest of the cell — including to the nucleus.

First characterised by Lee, Cohen and colleagues in 2015, MOTS-c was one of the first mitochondrial-derived peptides shown to have a defined metabolic signalling role, which is why it is of significant interest to researchers studying how mitochondria communicate their energy status to the cell.

AMPK and metabolic signalling

MOTS-c research centres on a single dominant pathway — AMPK activation — together with a striking mitochondrial-to-nuclear signalling mechanism and a set of metabolic outcomes studied in animal models.

AMPK activation via the folate cycle

The most studied property of MOTS-c is its activation of AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), the cell’s central energy sensor, which switches on when energy is low and promotes glucose uptake and fatty-acid oxidation. The proposed mechanism is indirect and elegant: MOTS-c interferes with the folate cycle and its tethered de novo purine biosynthesis, causing the AMPK-activating intermediate AICAR to accumulate, which in turn switches on AMPK. Skeletal muscle appears to be the primary target tissue in these studies.

Mitochondrial-to-nuclear signalling

Under metabolic stress, MOTS-c has been shown to translocate from the mitochondria to the cell nucleus, where it associates with stress-responsive transcription factors and helps regulate antioxidant and stress-defence gene programmes in an AMPK-dependent manner. This makes MOTS-c a rare, concrete example of a peptide that physically carries a signal from the mitochondria to the nucleus — a major reason it is studied as a model of mitochondrial-to-nuclear communication.

Insulin sensitivity, exercise and ageing

At the whole-animal level, MOTS-c treatment has been associated in rodent studies with prevention of age-dependent and high-fat-diet-induced insulin resistance and with reduced diet-induced obesity. It has also been described as an exercise-responsive peptide linked to physical capacity and age-related decline, positioning it as a research probe for metabolic-homeostasis and ageing pathways.

Key research findings

The following peer-reviewed studies are representative of the preclinical MOTS-c literature and are summarised for scientific reference only.

Metabolic homeostasis & AMPK
Lee C et al. — “The mitochondrial-derived peptide MOTS-c promotes metabolic homeostasis and reduces obesity and insulin resistance.” Cell Metabolism, 2015

The founding MOTS-c study: reported that the peptide targets skeletal muscle, activates AMPK via folate-cycle interference, and prevented age-dependent and high-fat-diet-induced insulin resistance and diet-induced obesity in mice.

PMID: 25738459

Mitochondrial-to-nuclear signalling
Kim KH et al. — “The mitochondrial-encoded peptide MOTS-c translocates to the nucleus to regulate nuclear gene expression in response to metabolic stress.” Cell Metabolism, 2018

Demonstrated that under metabolic stress MOTS-c moves to the nucleus and regulates nuclear stress-response and antioxidant gene expression in an AMPK-dependent manner — establishing it as a mitochondrial-to-nuclear signalling peptide.

PMID: 29983246

Research context
